You shipped something. It works. Now it needs a senior voice occasionally, and
hiring one for that is absurd while having nobody is uncomfortable.

## What a block of days covers

- **Reviews.** Architecture, code, and the pull request nobody feels senior
  enough to approve.
- **Escalations.** Something is behaving strangely and the team has been staring
  at it for a day.
- **Small builds.** The improvement that keeps getting deferred because it is
  half a sprint and nobody has half a sprint.
- **Roadmap input.** What to build next, and what to decommission.
- **Model and service updates.** New model versions, deprecations, and the
  question of whether a change is worth the regression testing it implies.

## What it is not

Not a support contract. There is no 24/7 rota, no incident response commitment,
and no compensation for downtime. Response is within agreed working hours on a
best-effort basis, and that is stated plainly here rather than discovered during
an outage. If you need an operations contract, you need an operations partner and
we will help you specify one.

## How the days work

A fixed number of days a month, agreed at the start. Unused days roll over within
the term under a rule written into the agreement, and do not accumulate for ever:
a retainer that becomes a savings account is one neither side is using properly.

Every quarter there is a short review of what the days were actually spent on. If
the pattern shows the retainer is the wrong instrument, we say so. Two outcomes
are common: it should be [fractional leadership](/engagements/fractional-leadership/)
because the team needs direction rather than reviews, or it should end because the
team has absorbed the work.

## Ending it

Notice period stated in the agreement, no exit fee, and a handover session
covering anything still open. Documentation stays current throughout rather than
being written at the end, so there is nothing to reconstruct.

## Commercials

Priced as a monthly block of days from the published hourly rate, with a reduction
that scales with the block size and the term. See
