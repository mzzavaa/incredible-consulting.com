For leaders who suspect the current architecture will not hold and need a read
that is not written by the people who built it or by the vendor who sold it.

## Scope options

One or more of:

- **Cloud architecture.** Account and network structure, identity, resilience,
  cost, operational readiness.
- **AI system.** Evaluation, guardrails, data flows, tool permissions, model and
  vendor dependency, cost per task.
- **Data platform.** Ingestion, modelling, quality, lineage, access, residency,
  cost per query.

## How it runs

**Read the system, not the diagram.** Infrastructure as code, application code,
configuration, dashboards and bills. The diagram is checked against reality rather
than trusted.

**Talk to the people who operate it.** Short interviews with the engineers on
call, the person who owns the budget, and whoever gets phoned when it breaks. The
gap between those three accounts is usually where the finding is.

**Look at the telemetry.** What actually fails, how often, and what it costs. Most
audits find at least one component that everyone assumes is load-bearing and is
not, and one that nobody mentions and is.

## What you get

- A written report with findings ranked by risk and by cost, each with the
  evidence behind it
- A remediation plan with effort estimates, so it can be budgeted rather than
  admired
- Two readouts: one with the engineering team, one with leadership, because they
  need different halves of the same document
- The things that are fine, stated explicitly. An audit that only lists problems
  is not an assessment, it is a sales document

## Access and confidentiality

Read-only access is enough for most of it. Where credentials are needed they are
scoped, time-limited, and issued by you. Findings are yours; nothing from the
engagement is reused as public material without written agreement. See
[Security and compliance](/faq/security-and-compliance/).

## What this is not

Not a penetration test, and not a formal certification audit. Where a finding
needs a specialist, the report says so and names the discipline rather than
bluffing it. Security testing is available through named partners in the
[associate network](/team/network/).
