For leadership and engineering teams that need alignment and a decision, not a
training course. Everyone leaves with the same understanding of what is being
built, for whom, and whether it is worth it.

Run as one day, or as two to three separate sessions with space between them so
that data can be checked and stakeholders consulted between rounds. Format
depends on the goal and on your people's availability, and is agreed in the free
first call.

## The three modules

**1. Goals and requirements. One to three hours.**
Develop the goals and requirements together, and find the use cases.
Result: a goal definition and a set of use cases with their users.
Perspective: what is the goal? Increase efficiency, reduce cost, or generate new
revenue?

**2. Data and feasibility. Two to four hours.**
Define the prototype out of managed services and identify the data it needs.
Result: a concept presentation, or a demo video.
Perspective: which data and which services? Data sources identified and tested,
not assumed.

**3. Need and implementation. One to two hours.**
Cost estimation for infrastructure and operation, with an ROI analysis.
Result: demo results and a roadmap with costs.
Perspective: what is the added value, against a clearly defined problem?

## Agenda for the first workshop

A typical first discovery workshop, one to three hours:

- **Goal definition and introduction, about 30 minutes.** Participants, goals,
  existing ideas or initiatives, and the potential users.
- **Use cases, about 1 hour.** Possible application scenarios, the components
  they need, the users and their processes.
- **Test data, about 1 hour.** Analysis or discussion of existing solutions, and
  of the data and technical fundamentals required.
- **Next steps, about 30 minutes.** Recap, next steps and milestones.

You leave with a common understanding of goals and requirements, and a written
goal definition with use cases and users.

## Who should be in the room

The person who owns the problem, someone who knows what the data actually looks
like, and someone who can say yes. Up to five participants is the assumption
behind the published price, and it is also the number at which a working session
stays a working session.

Who should not be in the room: anyone attending to observe. It changes how
honestly people talk about what is broken.

## What you get in writing

- Goal definition and ranked use cases
- Technical concept per use case, built from managed services
- Data and feasibility findings, including what is missing
- Cost estimate for infrastructure and operation
- A next-step recommendation, including the recommendation to stop

Each written result is one of the [results](/pricing/) in the pricing model, at
about EUR 1,000 each with one feedback round, or bundled into a
[package](/pricing/packages/).

## Where it leads

Straight into a [proof of concept](/engagements/proof-of-concept/) if the
feasibility work holds up, into an
[architecture audit](/engagements/architecture-audit/) if the problem turns out to
be an existing system rather than a missing one, or into a written
recommendation to stop. Ideas that are out of scope are parked in writing and
kept in the discussion rather than dropped.

If what the team actually needs is skills rather than a decision, that is
training, and it is sold on [incredible.training](https://incredible.training/)
rather than here.
