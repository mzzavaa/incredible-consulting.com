For founders and CTOs who need architecture decisions made, written down and
defended, without hiring a full-time principal engineer for a problem that will
not need one in eighteen months.

## What the role covers

- **Architecture ownership.** The decisions get made, recorded with their
  alternatives, and revisited when the reasons change.
- **Technical hiring support.** Writing the role honestly, sitting on the panel,
  and telling you when the person you like is wrong for the job.
- **Vendor and cloud conversations.** Reviewing proposals, pricing and contracts
  from the buyer's side, including the parts that are designed not to be read.
- **Oversight of external delivery.** Reviewing what an agency or implementation
  partner is producing, in code rather than in status reports.
- **Escalation.** The senior person who is already in context when something
  breaks.

## Cadence

Fixed days, agreed in advance and held. A standing architecture review, a written
decision record for anything consequential, and one channel where the team can
reach the role between days. Presence is scheduled rather than ambient: a
fractional lead who is theoretically always available is actually never available.

## What stays with your team

Everything. The point of the arrangement is that your engineers own the systems
and get better at owning them. Decisions are made in the open with the people who
will live with them, not delivered as verdicts.

## The exit is part of the deal

Written from the start: what has to be true for the role to end, and who takes
each part of it. Usually that is a senior hire, sometimes it is that the platform
stopped needing the attention. Either way the handover is planned rather than
improvised, and the decision records mean the reasoning does not leave with the
person.

## How this differs from the neighbours

- **Against a [retainer](/engagements/retainer/):** a retainer buys reactive
  capacity for a system that already works. This buys direction for one that is
  still being decided.
- **Against interim management:** an interim manager runs the team. This role
  owns the technical direction and does not take line management of your people.
- **Against a contractor:** a contractor takes tickets. This role decides which
  tickets should exist.
