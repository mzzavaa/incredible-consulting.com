**Purpose.** AI work sits on top of a delivery platform, and most of the time that platform is the
actual bottleneck. This page is for the reader whose problem is not a model, it is that shipping
anything takes three weeks and four tickets.

**Contains.**
- The position stated plainly: an internal developer platform is worth building when developer
  friction is measurable, and is an expensive vanity project when it is not. How we tell the
  difference before quoting
- **Internal developer portals.** Backstage and Red Hat Developer Hub, what each is good at, and the
  honest note that the portal is the easy part while the service catalogue and ownership model are
  the hard part
- **Configuration and automation.** Ansible for configuration management, and Ansible Automation
  Platform where an organisation needs governance, RBAC, and auditability around it rather than
  playbooks in someone's home directory
- **Container platforms.** OpenShift, deployed on AWS, on Azure, or on premises, and the tradeoff
  each target brings on cost, sovereignty, and operational burden
- Where this meets the AI work: agent systems need somewhere to run, a deployment path, and an
  owner. A team without those does not have an AI problem
- Delivery model: architecture and scoping in house, specialist delivery through the associate
  network, named before anyone joins the project
- What we do not do: staff augmentation, or dropping a platform in and leaving
- Reference architecture diagram slot
