Three engagements, written so that a sceptical engineer can tell whether the work
was real. Each states what the problem was, what was built, what it cost to run,
and what did not work.

## On naming clients

No client is named anywhere on this site, and no logo appears. That is not
modesty: displaying a client name or mark requires written permission, and it has
not been asked for yet. Sector and workload shape are described instead.

At proposal stage, for a serious buyer, a reference call can usually be arranged
with a client who has agreed to take one. That is a better signal than a logo
wall, and it is harder to fake.

## The three

- [**Conversational GIS for Grid Analysis**](/case-studies/geo-ai-platform/).
  Spatial analysis that queued behind a handful of specialists, opened up to
  domain experts through questions asked in plain German.
- [**Multi-Agent Automation Hub**](/case-studies/automation-hub/). Five
  half-automated back-office workflows consolidated behind one conversational
  entry point, with infrastructure as code and a deployment pipeline.
- [**Serverless Video Intelligence Pipeline**](/case-studies/video-intelligence-pipeline/).
  Hours of raw footage turned into publishable cuts without a manual editing
  pass, with validation to stop the agents inventing content.

## What is deliberately absent

No percentage improvements attributed to engagements that cannot support them. The
headline figures on this site are the ones that can be traced to a named piece of
work, which is why some of them are phrases rather than numbers. A number nobody
can substantiate is worse than an honest sentence.
