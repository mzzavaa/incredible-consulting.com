**Stated plainly first:** the experience here is adjacent rather than delivered.
Publicly funded research submissions, open methodology work, and community and
foundation involvement in Austria, plus the governance and sovereignty
architecture that public bodies require. There is no delivered public authority
engagement to reference.

## The constraints that shape everything

**Sovereignty is not negotiable.** Data residency, and often a requirement that
processing happens on infrastructure under public control. This decides the
architecture before any other requirement is considered, which is why the
self-hosted and open weight model question is a serious one here rather than an
ideological one.

**Procurement decides the timeline.** A thing that would take six weeks to build
takes nine months to buy. Design accordingly: smaller scopes, defensible award
criteria, and deliverables that stand alone rather than a monolith that has to be
bought in one piece.

**Transparency is an output.** Decisions have to be explainable to people who were
not in the room and who may be entitled to ask. Decision records and open
documentation are a requirement rather than good practice.

**Continuity beyond the supplier.** A system nobody in the authority can operate
is a failed project regardless of how well it works on delivery day. This raises
the weight of [handover](/method/handover/) and of avoiding lock-in that cannot be
exited.

## Where the AI Act lands hardest

Public bodies are deployers of AI systems in the sense the regulation means, and
several of the use cases that look most attractive, eligibility assessment,
prioritisation, resource allocation, are the ones carrying the heaviest
obligations. The useful work is classifying honestly, early, and designing the
oversight into the workflow rather than adding it after a review. See
[AI governance](/services/ai-governance/).

## Open by default

Methodology, playbooks and checklists are published openly and free, on
incredible-consulting.org, rather than sold as proprietary IP. For publicly funded
work that is the appropriate default, and it also means a public body can run the
method without buying anything.

## Where to start

A [workshop series](/engagements/workshop-series/), usually scoped small enough to
sit inside a direct award threshold, ending in a specification that can be
procured properly. Entity details and the vendor pack for procurement teams are on
