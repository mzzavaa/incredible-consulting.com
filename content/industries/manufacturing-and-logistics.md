**Stated plainly first:** there is no named manufacturing or logistics engagement
to point at yet. The operations automation patterns below are delivered work, in
adjacent settings. Judge the pattern, not the sector label.

## The problems that recur here

**Coordination, not tasks.** Order handling, supplier communication, quality
exceptions, returns and claims. Each individual step is usually fine. The cost
sits in the handovers: the approval waiting two days, the exception routed to a
person without the context, the same figure re-keyed into a third system.

**Documents as the interface between organisations.** Delivery notes, certificates,
customs paperwork, supplier declarations and specifications, arriving as PDFs and
scans because that is what the counterparty sends. Extraction is the easy half;
validation against what the system already believes is the half that matters.

**Long-lived systems that cannot be replaced.** An ERP that has been configured for
fifteen years is not going to be swapped because an AI project would prefer a
nicer API. Work has to fit around it, which usually means event-driven integration
at the edges rather than a migration.

**Data that exists but is not usable.** Machine and sensor data collected reliably
and stored in a form nobody can query, which is a data platform problem wearing an
AI costume.

## What transfers directly

The [Multi-Agent Automation Hub](/case-studies/automation-hub/) is the closest
delivered example: several coordination-heavy processes, each individually too
small to justify a project, consolidated behind one entry point with a shared
runtime, deployment pipeline and cost measurement. That pattern is sector-neutral,
and the argument for it is the same here.

Document workflows transfer the same way: extract, structure, validate, with a
validation step that fails loudly rather than passing a wrong number downstream.

## What does not transfer

Domain knowledge. What a plausible tolerance looks like, which exception is
routine and which is a recall, and what the regulator expects. That comes from
your side, which is why the workshop format puts your operations people in the
room rather than interviewing them afterwards.

## Where to start

A [workshop series](/engagements/workshop-series/) that measures where the hours
actually go before proposing to automate anything. In this sector the ranking step
usually eliminates more candidates than it approves, and that is the useful part.
