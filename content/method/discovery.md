Failed AI projects are rarely lost in the build. They are lost here, quietly, by
starting work on a problem nobody had defined and finding out in month four that
the data was never going to support it.

## What happens

**Interviews with the people who own the problem.** Not a survey. Short
conversations with the person who feels the pain, the person who knows what the
data really looks like, and the person who controls the budget. The three accounts
rarely agree, and the disagreement is the first finding.

**A data reality check.** What exists, what is usable, and what is a rumour. Field
by field, for the fields that matter. This is the step that most often changes the
plan, and doing it late is what makes projects expensive.

**Constraint mapping.** Regulatory, technical and organisational. The constraint
that kills a project is usually organisational and is usually known by someone who
was not invited to the first meeting.

**Use case ranking.** Value against feasibility, scored openly so the ranking can
be argued with. Where a client arrives with dozens of candidate use cases, the
work is triage before it is analysis.

**The success metric.** Written down, in numbers, agreed by the person who
controls the budget. If nobody will commit to a number, that is itself the
finding, and the recommendation is to stop until somebody will.

## The gap is deliberate

Between the discovery session and the next one there is a pause, usually one to
two weeks. It is where your team gathers sample data, confirms access, and
surfaces the legal and security constraints that only appear once something is
requested for real. The gap is not slack in the schedule. It is the step that
turns assumptions into inputs, and compressing it out is the single most reliable
way to make the following phase useless.

## What you get

- Ranked use cases with the scoring visible
- The agreed success metric and how it will be measured
- Target architecture sketch, at the level of managed services rather than boxes
- Effort estimates per candidate
- A written recommendation, including the option to stop

## The honest outcome

A meaningful share of discovery engagements end with a recommendation not to
build. That is a good result delivered for a few thousand euro instead of a bad
one delivered for six figures.
