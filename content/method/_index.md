There is no discovery phase that quietly consumes six weeks, and there is no
deck at the end of it. The method has one shape: get to something people can look
at as early as possible, then decide with evidence instead of opinion.

## The three workshops

Most engagements start here. One day, or two to three separate sessions with
space between them, depending on the goal and on how much of your organisation
needs to be in the room.

**Workshop one: goal and impact. One to three hours.**
Develop the goals and the requirements together. What is the goal: efficiency,
cost, or new revenue? Who needs this most? The output is a written goal
definition and a set of use cases with the users named.

**Workshop two: data and feasibility. Two to four hours.**
Define the prototype out of managed services, and identify and test the data
sources it needs. This is where an idea usually either survives or is found to
depend on data nobody actually has. The output is a technical concept per use
case, presented or recorded as a demo video.

**Workshop three: need and implementation. One to two hours.**
Cost estimation for infrastructure and operation, with an ROI analysis. What is
the added value, against a clearly defined problem? The output is the demo
result and a roadmap with costs attached.

The [workshop series](/engagements/workshop-series/) page has the agenda for the
first session, hour by hour.

## The results ladder

Every engagement is somewhere on the same ladder, and the point of the method is
to move one rung at a time rather than promising the top of it in the first
meeting.

1. **Clarity.** People understand the idea.
2. **Concept.** People believe the idea.
3. **Prototype or demo.** People can try the idea. Not functional: a
   works-on-my-machine setup, on your data.
4. **MVP or proof of concept.** People use the idea. Functional, built with your
   team or an implementation partner.
5. **Functional product.** People rely on the idea. Monetisable, operated,
   scaled.

The first three rungs are what the workshop packages deliver. The last two are
built with your team or a partner, with us in a delivery or leadership role. The
boundary is stated because it is where most consulting promises quietly break.

## The four delivery phases

Once a build is agreed, the engagement runs in four phases with a go or stop
decision between each one.

- [**1. Discovery**](/method/discovery/). The problem, the constraints, and the
  success metric, in writing and in numbers.
- [**2. Prototype**](/method/prototype/). The smallest thing that proves the
  answer, with evaluation attached from day one.
- [**3. Production**](/method/production/). Infrastructure as code,
  observability, guardrails, security review, and a cost model that holds.
- [**4. Handover**](/method/handover/). Documentation, a recorded walkthrough,
  and an ownership map, so your team owns the system.

## The rules that make it work

- **Something running, every week.** A demo, not a status report.
- **A written decision trail.** Every significant choice recorded with the
  alternatives and the reason, so it can be reviewed by someone who was not in
  the room.
- **Kill criteria agreed up front.** The conditions under which we recommend
  stopping are written down before the work starts, and stopping is a legitimate
  outcome.
- **Your repository from the first commit.** Not a handover at the end.
- **Ideas parked, not lost.** Anything out of scope is written down and kept in
  the discussion rather than argued about mid-session.

## What we need from you

A decision maker who can say yes, access to the data or a written explanation of
why it is not available, and one person from your side who is genuinely available
during the build. Engagements fail on the third one more often than on the
first two.
