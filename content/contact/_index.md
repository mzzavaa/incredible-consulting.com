Three ways in, depending on how far along you are.

## Book a call

A free 30 minute idea call with the person who would do the work. Best when you
have a problem and no agreed scope, which is most of the time. See
[Book a call](/contact/book-a-call/).

## Request a proposal

For teams with a defined scope who need a written proposal, a price and a start
date. Skips the exploratory stage. See
[Request a proposal](/contact/request-a-proposal/).

## Just write

Email works and reaches the same person. No form on this page, deliberately: this
site collects nothing, sets no cookies, and runs no tracking, and a contact form
would change all three. See [Privacy](/legal/privacy/).

## What happens after you get in touch

1. **You get a reply within two working days,** from the person who would run the
   work, not from an assistant.
2. **If it is a fit, a call is booked.** Thirty minutes, free.
3. **A short written summary follows,** with a recommended engagement model and a
   rough number using the published [prices](/pricing/).
4. **If you want it, a proposal follows** with scope, deliverables, price,
   assumptions, exclusions and a start date.
5. **If it is not a fit, you are told that,** with a pointer to who would be
   better, where we know one.

## What to include in a first message

The problem in one or two sentences. What has already been tried. Any hard
constraint you know about: a deadline, a regulator, a system that cannot be
touched, a budget ceiling. That is enough for a useful first call. If you have
none of it, come anyway; working out what the constraint is can be the whole value
of the conversation.
