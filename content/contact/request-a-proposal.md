For buyers past the exploratory stage. Send the brief and you get a proposal,
without another call first.

There is no form on this page. This site collects nothing and runs no tracking,
and adding a form would change that. Email the brief instead: it reaches the same
person a form would have, faster.

## What to send

The more of this you have, the better the proposal. Missing items are fine; say
they are missing rather than guessing.

- **The problem.** What is happening now, and what should be happening instead.
- **The outcome.** What has to be true for this to have been worth doing, ideally
  as a number.
- **Current stack.** Cloud provider, key systems, and anything that cannot be
  changed.
- **The data.** What exists, where it lives, and who controls access.
- **Constraints.** Regulatory, contractual, technical, organisational. Include the
  political ones; they are usually the binding ones.
- **Timeline.** Real dates and what drives them. A board meeting and a budget
  year-end are different constraints.
- **Budget range.** Not a negotiating position. It determines the shape of the
  proposal, and a range is enough.
- **Decision process.** Who signs, who has to be convinced, and what procurement
  requires.

An existing requirements document, RFP or architecture diagram can be attached
instead of writing any of the above again.

## What comes back

A written proposal containing:

- Scope, as a list of what is included
- Deliverables, each one a thing you can hold
- Price, fixed where the scope allows, with the assumptions it rests on
- **Exclusions**, stated explicitly. This is the section worth reading first
- Timeline and a start date
- The named lead, and any associate, by name
- Whether the work looks eligible for
  [cloud provider funding](/pricing/funding/)

Turnaround is within five working days. It is free, and it is written by the
person who would do the work, which is why it takes days rather than hours.

## RFPs and public tenders

Formal tenders are answered where the requirement is a genuine fit and the process
is proportionate to the size of the work. Where it is not, you get a straight no
rather than a compliant response nobody believes. Entity details, contract stack
and the vendor pack are on
[Procurement and contracting](/pricing/procurement/).

## Confidentiality

An NDA can be signed before anything is shared, usually yours as-is. Nothing sent
is used as public material without written permission. See
[Security and compliance](/faq/security-and-compliance/).

## If the scope is not actually defined

That is common and it is not a problem, but a proposal written against a vague
scope is a bad proposal. Book the [free call](/contact/book-a-call/) instead and
the scope comes out of that.
