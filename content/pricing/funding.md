Starting a cloud or AI project does not have to be expensive, and a surprising
number of teams pay full price for work that a provider would have partly funded.
Public cloud providers run funding programmes precisely because a successful
proof of concept tends to become consumption later. That incentive is worth using.

## What is available

**Financial support.** Cloud credits against infrastructure, and in several
programmes a cash contribution toward the consulting work itself, paid to the
partner delivering it rather than reimbursed to you.

**Expertise.** Access to provider solution architects alongside the engagement,
at no cost, which is genuinely useful on an unusual architecture.

**Training and tools.** Enablement for your team during the implementation, so
the system does not arrive without anyone able to run it.

## The two bands

**Smaller funding, up to about EUR 10,000.** Pilot projects, first cloud
workloads, targeted modernisation steps. This is the band most proofs of concept
fall into, and it frequently covers a large share of a
[package](/pricing/packages/).

**Larger funding, about EUR 50,000 to EUR 500,000.** Migration of whole systems
and larger modernisation programmes. Migration funding in practice has supported
engagements up to roughly EUR 400,000. These programmes carry real obligations:
a defined scope, a timeline, milestone evidence, and reporting.

## How it works in an engagement

1. **Eligibility check during scoping.** Before the proposal, we check which
   programme the work could fit and what it would require from you.
2. **Application written with you.** The provider wants a scope, a timeline, an
   expected outcome and a consumption estimate. Most of that already exists as
   the workshop output, which is why the sequencing matters.
3. **Approval before the work starts.** Funding is applied for and confirmed
   before delivery begins wherever the programme allows it. Retroactive
   applications usually fail.
4. **Evidence during delivery.** Milestones, and at the end a short report. This
   is part of the engagement, not additional paperwork billed to you.

## The honest caveats

- **Funding is never guaranteed.** These are provider programmes with eligibility
  criteria, budgets and deadlines that change. Nothing on this page is a promise
  of an approval.
- **It is paid against work, not as cash to you.** Cash contributions generally
  flow to the delivery partner and reduce what you pay. Credits offset
  infrastructure, not fees.
- **It creates a direction of travel.** Provider funding pulls an architecture
  toward that provider. If genuine platform neutrality matters for a decision,
  that decision should be made before a funding application, and we will say so.
- **It has strings.** Larger programmes require milestone reporting and a
  committed scope. If your project is likely to change shape, a smaller
  unencumbered engagement is the better choice.
