## Why are the prices published?

Because the alternative wastes both sides' time. A buyer who cannot afford this
should find that out on the website, not in the third meeting. See
[Pricing](/pricing/).

## What are the actual numbers?

About EUR 2,000 per workshop session of two to four hours, including preparation,
research and documentation. About EUR 1,000 per result document, including one
feedback round. EUR 250 per hour for ad hoc work. Packages from about EUR 4,000 to
about EUR 16,000. All net, excluding VAT, valid for 2026.

## What is included in a session fee?

Preparation, the research behind it, the facilitation itself, written
documentation of what was decided, and a next-step recommendation. It is not an
hourly charge for time in the room, which is why a two-hour session and a
four-hour session cost the same.

## What moves a price up?

More than five participants in a workshop, more than three use cases in a single
result document, several stakeholder organisations in one room, a domain needing
significant preparatory research, or an environment where access is slow. Each is
named in the proposal rather than discovered on the invoice.

## Fixed fee or time and materials?

Fixed fee wherever the scope can be written down, which is most things: workshops,
results, audits, proofs of concept, defined builds. Time and materials for
genuinely open-ended work, priced from the hourly rate, with an estimate and a
checkpoint rather than an open tap.

## What happens if the estimate is wrong?

On fixed-fee work, an underestimate is ours to absorb: that is what the buyer is
paying for. What changes the fee is a change of scope, not a change of difficulty,
and the assumptions the fee rests on are listed in the statement of work so it is
clear which has happened.

If it becomes clear early that the scope was wrong, you hear that early, with the
options, rather than at the end.

## Is there a deposit?

Packages are invoiced half at the start and half on delivery of the last result.
Longer engagements are invoiced monthly in arrears against delivered work. Payment
terms are net 14 days.

## Do you do discounts?

Duration and volume are reflected in retainer and fractional pricing, where the
reduction scales with the size and length of the commitment. Non-profit, community
and public-interest work is quoted at a reduced rate. There is no discount for
signing quickly, because that is a sales tactic rather than a price.

## Does a workshop fee count toward a build?

Yes. If a package turns into a larger engagement, the package fee is credited
against the first invoice of that engagement. There is no penalty for stopping
instead, which is a legitimate outcome and one of the reasons the packages are
priced the way they are.

## Who pays for infrastructure?

You do, directly, on your own cloud account. Licence, infrastructure and model
inference costs are yours and are never marked up here. The estimate for them is
one of the deliverables.

## Can a cloud provider pay for part of this?

Often, yes. Cash contributions of up to about EUR 10,000 per proof of concept, and
programmes from about EUR 50,000 to EUR 500,000 for migration and modernisation.
The eligibility check happens in the first call, and applying is part of the
engagement rather than an extra. It is never guaranteed. See
[Cloud funding and credits](/pricing/funding/).

## What is never charged for?

The first call, the proposal, travel within Vienna, and the correction of our own
mistakes.
