Linda Mohamed is the founder of Incredible Consulting and the named lead on every
engagement this site sells. She has spent more than twelve years in software
development and IT, six of them building on cloud and AI, and six inside
enterprise IT consulting before going independent. The work has run from writing
production code, through architecture and platform roles, to facilitating the
workshops where a company decides what is worth building at all.

She is an AWS Community Hero, a recognition AWS gives to a small number of
practitioners worldwide for sustained technical contribution to the community
rather than for sales or certification volume. She organises the AWS User Group
in Vienna, supports the Förderverein AWS Community DACH, and speaks and runs
workshops at community and industry events.

## What she actually does on an engagement

Most of the value in an AI or cloud project is decided before anyone opens an
editor. The problem is usually not the model or the service, it is that nobody
has written down which problem is being solved, which data exists, who will use
the result, and what number has to move for the project to have been worth doing.

On an engagement that means:

- **Facilitation.** Running the sessions where the goal, the users and the
  use cases get defined, and where the awkward questions get asked while they
  are still cheap to answer.
- **Architecture.** Designing the target system, choosing managed services
  over bespoke code wherever that holds, and writing down the trade-off so the
  decision can be reviewed later.
- **Building.** Writing the prototype, the pipeline or the agent system,
  in your repository, from the first commit.
- **Cost and funding.** Estimating what the production version costs to run
  before it is built, and checking whether the work qualifies for cloud provider
  funding. See [Cloud funding and credits](/pricing/funding/).
- **Handover.** Documentation, a recorded walkthrough, and an ownership map,
  so the system does not depend on her being available.

## Where she is strongest

Deepest on AWS: Bedrock and agent runtimes, serverless data and media pipelines,
event driven automation, and the cost engineering that keeps any of it
affordable. Comfortable on hybrid and self hosted setups, including OpenShift and
open weight models, when data residency or cost at volume makes public cloud AI
the wrong answer. Azure and Google Cloud depth comes from the
[associate network](/team/network/) rather than from her personally, and this
site says so rather than claiming equal mastery everywhere.

Before going independent the work was mostly other people's products, in
employed and consulting roles: mobile applications for telecommunications sales
teams, an internal booking system that replaced a hardware rollout nobody could
afford, a live data product for sports events, the migration of a multi-channel
media platform from a monolith to services, a smart home integration built
against reverse-engineered APIs, and audio and video analytics pipelines on AWS.
Different domains, one recurring pattern: the interesting problem was rarely the
technology. The full record, with dates and roles, is on the personal site rather
than here.

Two working languages, German and English. Workshops are usually run in German
for teams in Austria and Germany, and documentation is delivered in whichever
language the receiving team reads.

## Three things worth knowing

1. **She builds prototypes.** The default answer to a disagreement about whether
   something will work is to build the smallest version of it and look at the
   result, rather than to hold another meeting.
2. **She is a process nerd.** Decision records, quality gates, and a written
   trail behind every choice. This is what makes an engagement auditable
   afterwards, and it is also why the fourth phase, handover, is scoped as real
   work rather than a goodbye email.
3. **She juggles.** Literally, as a hobby, and it is why the photograph on the
   homepage is an empty conference hall with three balls in the air.
