Teams turn up here with one of two problems. Either they have an AI or cloud
ambition and no agreed scope, or they have a pilot that impressed nobody and need
to know whether the idea or the execution was wrong.

Both are solved the same way: turn the ambition into a small number of concrete
use cases, build the smallest thing that answers the hardest question, and decide
with evidence. What follows is the shape that work takes, grouped by the
discipline it mostly lives in. Most real engagements cross two of them.

## The five practices

- [**AI and agent systems**](/services/ai-and-agents/). Multi-agent systems,
  retrieval and document intelligence, and LLM applications taken from a
  prototype to something you can operate.
- [**Cloud architecture**](/services/cloud-architecture/). AWS and hybrid
  architecture, migrations, cost control, and reviews from someone who has run
  the systems rather than only drawn them.
- [**Automation**](/services/automation/). The manual work between your systems:
  internal workflows, document handling, and the operations that still run on
  copy and paste.
- [**Data platforms**](/services/data-platforms/). Pipelines, storage and
  modelling that make data usable for analytics and for the AI built on top of
  it.
- [**AI governance**](/services/ai-governance/). EU AI Act readiness, model risk
  documentation, and the audit trail your legal team will ask for.

## Where the work actually clusters

Four problem shapes come up again and again, across very different industries:

1. **Agents and process automation.** Coordination-heavy processes with
   handoffs, approvals and internal operations, where the bottleneck is the
   handover rather than any single step.
2. **Document and knowledge analysis.** Extract, structure and validate:
   contracts, compliance documents, and the knowledge that currently lives in
   somebody's inbox.
3. **Geospatial and satellite analysis.** Spatial questions that today require a
   specialist and a desktop GIS client.
4. **Video and media pipelines.** Large volumes of footage that have to be
   analysed, selected and assembled faster than a person can watch them.

If your problem is one of these, the path is short. If it is not, the discovery
workshop exists precisely to work out whether it is a real use case or an
expensive detour.

## Not sure which one

Most buyers are not, and it does not matter. The
[workshop series](/engagements/workshop-series/) starts from the problem rather
than the practice, and the first call is free. The most common outcome of a
first workshop is the discovery that the interesting problem was not the one in
the original brief.

## What is not sold here

Staff augmentation. Unmanaged offshore delivery. AI strategy decks without
validation. Vendor-locked implementations. Long-term operations of a system your
team should own.

Training, as opposed to delivery, is a different thing entirely and is sold on
[incredible.training](https://incredible.training/) rather than here. The
difference: training sells capability, so your people can build it; consulting
sells delivery, so you get a working system.
